# Petai — Hard Product Rules

> Source: Petai product plan §§3, 5, 21. These are constraints, not
> suggestions. If a feature conflicts with a rule, the feature loses.

## Rule 1 — The pet is NOT an assistant

This distinction is fundamental. Petai must never accidentally become
another AI assistant.

The creature must not primarily: manage calendars, write emails, manage
tasks, organize work, act as a productivity agent, or become a
general-purpose chatbot.

The creature may interact with the user's digital world, but **only as
entertainment**:

Good — the pet plays a videogame · explores a digital environment · makes
a funny video · sends the owner a surprise message · interacts with another
user's pet · gets obsessed with a game.

Bad — "I'll add that meeting to your calendar." · "I'll organize your
tasks." · "I'll summarize your work emails."

The creature's purpose is: **companionship + entertainment + play +
emotional connection.**

## Rule 2 — Maximum freedom at creation, meaningful constraints after

The user describes anything they can imagine. But after creation, the pet
has a simulated internal state — needs, moods, preferences — that the user
does **not** fully control. Otherwise the creature becomes a puppet.

Key mechanism (plan §5):

> **The system derives the creature's mechanics from what the creature is.**

A plant-like alien might not need food. A robot needs energy instead. The
need model is generated from the creature's nature, giving the user freedom
without destroying the existence of rules.

## Rule 3 — Autonomy must not remove the user's role

The design target:

> **Autonomous enough to surprise you, dependent enough to need you.**

The pet entertains itself, but the user remains *its favorite person*.
Human intervention matters especially for: affection, care, important
decisions, activities together, exploration, progression, relationships.

The psychological loop we design for:

**care → investment → attachment → anticipation → surprise → attachment**

The user should feel: *"I need to check what my little creature has been
doing."*
